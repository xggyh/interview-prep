# 🟡 G21 · Tell me about a time you mentored a colleague.

> **信号**: 培养他人 · 乘数效应 · **hellointerview 报告数**: 2

## 🧠 这题在测什么

与 Q9 区分: Q9 是单技能教学; 这题给成长弧线 (能力→ownership→独当一面).

## 📋 English Answer (背诵稿, ~60-90 秒)

When a junior engineer joined our team, he had solid ML fundamentals but had never touched a production agent system — and the gap that actually blocks people isn't knowledge, it's judgment under production constraints.
I structured the mentorship as a progression of ownership rather than a curriculum. First month: he shadowed my incident reviews and design discussions, with one rule — he had to write down his own decision before hearing mine, then we compared. That builds judgment fast because every gap between his call and mine became a concrete conversation. Second phase: he owned one workflow end-to-end — design, eval cases, launch review — with me as reviewer, not driver. I deliberately let him take a small, recoverable failure in staging rather than catching everything, because a safe failure teaches more than ten warnings.
Within half a year he independently owned one market's chatbot workflows [✏️ 时间核实], and ran his own launch reviews. The proudest moment was hearing him push back on *my* design with eval data — the exact behavior I'd been modeling.
My mentoring principle: transfer ownership in slices, let safe failures happen, and measure success by when they stop needing you.

## 🇨🇳 中文速记 (结构记忆)

Junior 有 ML 基础缺生产判断力. 渐进所有权: ①影子期—先写下自己的决定再听我的, 比差距 ②独立 own 一个 workflow, 我只 review, 故意放行一次 staging 可恢复失败 ③半年独立 own 一个市场. 最自豪: 他拿 eval 数据反驳我的设计. 原则: 切片转移所有权, 允许安全失败, 以'不再需要你'为成功.

## 🔁 高概率追问

**Q: How do you mentor without slowing your own work?**

The before/after-decision journal costs me minutes but compounds for him — I optimize for high-leverage mentoring artifacts, not hours.
