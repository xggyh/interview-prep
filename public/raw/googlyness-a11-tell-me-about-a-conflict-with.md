## 🧠 这题在测什么

## 📋 English Answer (背诵稿, ~60-90 秒)

Marketing wanted the BNPL chatbot to proactively pitch PayLater in every conversation, whatever the user asked. I pushed back: in several of our markets the regulatory posture required BNPL promotion to be opt-in, and bolting sales onto a support conversation risked the user trust the bot depended on.
But I didn't open with "no." I opened with their goal — they were under real conversion pressure, and that was legitimate. So I proposed making the disagreement empirical: a two-week A/B. The hard-upsell variant did lift conversion short-term, but churn and complaint signals moved the wrong way, and compliance flagged the passive-promotion pattern [✏️ 数字核实].
I brought back the data *with* an alternative that still served their goal: contextual suggestion — surfacing PayLater only when the user's own question made it relevant. They took it; the soft variant kept most of the lift without the trust and compliance cost.
What actually resolved it: treating their objective as valid, converting opinion into experiment, and offering an option where they didn't have to lose.

## 🇨🇳 中文速记 (结构记忆)

Marketing 要每轮硬推 PayLater vs 监管 opt-in 红线; 先认可其转化压力 → 2 周 A/B; 硬推短期转化↑但 churn/投诉/合规↓; 带数据+替代案 (上下文相关才推荐). 解法=认可目标+经验化分歧+给不输的选项.

## 🔁 高概率追问

**Q: What if the A/B had favored hard upsell everywhere?**

The compliance line still binds — I'd have said upfront that metrics tune the tradeoff but regulatory red lines aren't A/B-testable.
