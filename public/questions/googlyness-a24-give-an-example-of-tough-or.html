🧠 这题在测什么

测三件: 当下反应 / 是否真改了 / 改变是否制度化. 别选伪缺点.

📋 English Answer (背诵稿, ~60-90 秒)

After one of my early internal talks on our LLM strategy for payments, a business stakeholder gave me feedback that stung: "Technically impressive. I have no idea what you want me to do with it." And a colleague added, more bluntly, that I was explaining architecture to people who needed to hear outcomes — that my talks served my expertise, not the audience's decisions. My first reaction was defensive — the content was correct. But correct-and-useless is still useless, and I knew it landed because I'd seen the glazed eyes myself. I rebuilt how I communicate upward and outward: every business-facing talk now opens with the decision I'm asking for and the business metric it moves, the architecture demoted to an appendix for whoever wants it. I started pressure-testing decks with one non-engineer before presenting. The shift compounded — I became a regular internal speaker, and the later strategy sessions directly shaped roadmap decisions [✏️ 细节核实] in a way the early ones never did. What I keep from it: expertise that doesn't transfer is a private hobby. The feedback hurt precisely because it was about the gap between being right and being useful — and that gap was mine to close.

🇨🇳 中文速记 (结构记忆)

业务方反馈: '技术很强, 但我不知道该拿它做什么决定' — 讲架构给需要听结论的人. 先防御后承认; 重建沟通: 业务场合开场先讲'要你做的决定+影响的业务指标', 架构进附录; deck 先过一个非工程师. 后果: 成为常驻内部 speaker, 策略分享真正影响 roadmap. 金句: 不可转移的专业是私人爱好.

🔁 高概率追问

Q: How do you seek feedback now?

I ask for it scoped and early — 'was the ask clear in the first two minutes?' — because vague 'any feedback?' invites politeness, not truth.
